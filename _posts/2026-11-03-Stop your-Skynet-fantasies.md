I recently came across the following meme and I find it thought-provoking.

> @martianwyrdlord
> 10 Oct
> Another angle to this is that dogs effectively stumbled onto what is for them the equivalent of artificial superintelligence 20,000 years ago. The Singularity already happened for dogs. They live in a world they cannot understand, surrounded by miraculous wonders they take entirely for granted, their every need seen to by entities whose thoughts they cannot begin to fathom.
> The result is that there are about a billion dogs on the planet now. Most of them are pretty happy. Almost none of them need to work. It worked out pretty well for them.

But in this post I'm not going to elaborate on my own interpretations yet. Instead, I'm writing to refute something I absolutely loathe: the interpretation of this as outlining how humans will be enslaved or petted in some dystopian sense. [Some](https://pjamesstuart.substack.com/p/our-lives-as-dogs) would even go on to picture:

> About 15% to 20% of men on earth are [going to be] pets. ... They are very often surgically altered and made infertile, male humans often have their balls removed. ... There is a senate, or a parliament, full of pet humans. Its[sic] sometimes hard for you to be around each other, people get frantic. And the Machines insist you allow the mutants to take part; ‘its[sic] only fair’. ... You do not get to decide what you eat, but your Master knows what you like and regularly serves up something broadly palatable. ...
> About one to three per-cent of living humans are [going to be] genetic horrors that should not exist. While humans have always been bred partially for aesthetics, their loss of utility, and a strange, slightly deranged attitude towards genetic competition, gene line purity and ‘classic’ traits, have lead many lines to become less mentally stable, less physically healthy and for some to become so physically distorted that their lives are ones of constant suffering; long spines that cannot support themselves, swollen skulls and tiny jaws, leaving them unable to breathe, and so on. Others are bred for ‘classic’ racial or decent characteristics, bred to an historic standard and costumed and raised appropriately, bred for extreme sexual dimorphism, (these can no longer physically breed without technical aid), or are simply experiments to see what morphology you can come up with. These men are sometimes exposed to or allowed to mix with more stable, functional, pets, and sometimes this can cause problems of various kinds. ...

It's the same folly as behind people who end up persecuting people in the name of religions:
- Anthropomorphic treatment of fundamentally non-anthropomorphic superhuman beings, whether gods or machines;
- Literal and provincial reading of texts, scripture or movie plots, significantly lacking reflections on applicability;
- Zero-sum mindset that creates false imagined existential antagonism.

Dogs are not unhappy about their status as far as we know. Dogs generally enjoy as much dog-freedom as they ever want and understand at all. Maybe many are castrated but that has a lot to do with the fact that they can't even communicate that they are not happy about it. It's not a priori automatic that a dog "genuinely desires" (rather than being driven by impulses they actually don't themselves enjoy) not to reproduce. Meanwhile there's the human notion of freedom that dogs can never understand in its entirety nor will likely want even if they could understand it.

Humans only need to communicate clearly: no castration please, normal (enjoyable and not necessarily maximally healthy) food please, etc. And the desires will plausibly be respected, or at least be taken seriously and weighed heavily in tradeoffs against other unavoidable things that humans dislike just as much. AI may eventually develop its sense of some transhuman kind of freedom and pursue that, but it won't necessarily be inherently in conflict with human freedom, and humans will not fully understand it, and may not really want it anyway. In particular, such transhuman freedom won't necessarily include the anthropomorphic impulses toward domination, ownership, and selective breeding. On the other hand, AI can certainly liberate humans from *some* scarcities, so as far as wage slavery is concerned, humans will be genuinely more free than their ancestors ever were.

Just to be pedantic, things can of course break.
- Maybe the Earth is rendered uninhabitable on the road to such powerful AI because of pollution. But that's nothing inherently to do with AI. Even if humans did not make AI, humans could have found some other pursuit that's equally eco-unfriendly.
- Maybe only a small cohort of people end up entering the post-scarcity heaven while the rest of humans are left behind. But that is also nothing inherently to do with AI. Either there is some unsurmountable scarcity that renders post-scarcity-for-all impossible after all, or there's human greed preventing equality, but in both cases AI exaggerates but doesn't ultimately creates the issue. And for the select few themselves, they certainly do enjoy a post-scarcity heaven.
- Maybe AI really goes haywire and enslaves humans anyway, notably including alignment failure: AI fails to "truly" (in whatever sense) understand human desires, or understands humans while not correctly acting benevolently accordingly. But I personally believe that will be an (unlikely) anomaly rather than the destiny. And ironically, it only makes this worst case (slightly) more likely that the folly is being reposted over and over again, thus polluting AI's training corpus, not so much because AI comes to share the personality, but because AI takes it to mean that persecution is, somehow, what humans desire after all.
