TL;DR: For a 1-D form $df$, it is impossible to assign a unit. However, we can still assign informative types to mappings. We further discuss how units are practically a special case of typing.

A glaring omission from differential geometry, or indeed much of mathematics, education is the use of units. Yes, professional mathematicians can make a living entirely without ever needing to put values in units. Whether a scalar is inherently count-like (intuitively dimensionless), length-like (intuitively like $\mathrm{meter}$), or metric-like (intuitively like $\mathrm{meter}^{-1}$), mathematicians often just leave it a dimensionless value. However, especially in disciplines like differential geometry where values often inherently stand for "lengths" or "volumes" or "rates of change," it will be helpful if beginners can keep track of values in terms of units, which helps connect day-to-day geometric intuition to the abstractions.
